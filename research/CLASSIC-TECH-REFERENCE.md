# Reference: how the 1998 classic paces tech, upgrades and expansions

**Research only.** Nothing here goes into Driftfall by name, number or art. This file records the *structure* of the
classic so Driftfall can copy the principles (see TERRITORY.md for our original design). Times are in-game time on
the fastest speed. Most come from published pro build orders; treat them as typical, not exact.

## 1. The shape of a game

| Phase | Game time | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 0:00 – ~3:00 | Workers nonstop, first production building, first gas, first scout. A first expansion (the "natural") goes down anywhere from ~1:30 (greedy) to ~3:30 (safe). |
| Early game | ~3:00 – ~6:00 | Tier 2 comes online (~4:00–5:00). The first unit upgrade lands (speed or range, ~5:00–6:30). First timing attacks. |
| Mid game | ~6:00 – ~12:00 | A third base (~7:00–9:00), +1 weapons/armor, 2–4 production buildings, Tier 3 (~9:00–12:00). Fights over expansions. |
| Late game | 12:00 + | 4+ bases, upgrades toward level 3, top-tier units, the map runs out of resources and the fight is over the last fields. |

## 2. Tiers

Each race has the same skeleton, built differently:
- **Tier 1** (from the start): base, workers, supply, one basic production building, cheap units, static defence.
- **Tier 2** (needs one "core" tech building, ~4:00–5:00): gas-heavy units, mobility, the first spells, the
  upgrade buildings. For some races the main building upgrades too.
- **Tier 3** (needs a second tech building on top of tier 2, ~9:00–12:00): capital units, strongest spells,
  stealth and detection tools, the final upgrade levels.
- Higher tiers also **unlock upgrade levels 2 and 3** of weapons and armor (level 1 needs only the upgrade building).

## 3. Upgrades (kinds, not names)

- **Numeric:** weapons and armor, 3 levels each, per unit class (infantry / vehicles / air, or melee / ranged). Same
  effect each level, cost rises each level, armor costs more than weapons. Some races add a shield track.
- **Per-unit "unlockers":** speed (+~50% move), range (+1 to +7 tiles), capacity (more ammo, more cargo), energy
  (+50 max energy), sight, and ability unlocks (stim, mines, siege mode, cloak, morph into a new unit).
- Typical cost of a unit upgrade: 100/100 to 200/200 resources, 50–105 seconds of research.
- A few upgrades **define the timing of a whole strategy**: the moment the speed or range upgrade finishes is when an
  army can leave home, and the opponent plays around that clock.

## 4. Sample timings from real build orders

**Speed-melee attack off a fast expand:** production building 3:05, gas 3:21, tier-2 core 3:46, first unit 3:57, air
building ~4:45, **+1 weapons 5:09**, speed building 5:19, speed research 6:05, **move out 6:44** with 5 units, hits at
6:54, tier-3 building 6:58, extra production 7:24, stealth switch 7:40.

**The defending side's window:** a greedy early expand cannot make its ranged defender until ~6:55 and has 4 by
7:08. A slower opening has enough to defend by ~7:31. That ~30-second gap is the "timing window" the attack above is
built to hit.

**One-base tech into a 2-base all-in (8 siege units + 4 raiders):** factory at 16 supply, expansion at 27 supply,
two economy "cuts" for siege mode, upgrades and 3–4 factories, attack at **~8:00 and ~60 supply**. If it doesn't do
enough damage, the attacker loses: the economy was spent on the army.

**Six-base swarm:** first production at 9 supply, expansion at 11, third base at 15, tier 2 at 18, speed at 21,
**4th, 5th and 6th bases at 32–38 supply**, ranged unit building at 40, +1 ranged and air armor at 44–47, mass ranged
units at ~50 supply. The whole build is: take territory early, defend it with static defence and a few units, then
flood.

## 5. What the classic actually teaches (the principles we keep)

1. **Every minute is a trade between economy, army and tech.** Greed (expand, tech) is punished by attacks. Attacks
   are punished by greed if they fail.
2. **Timings are public knowledge.** Both players know roughly when each building and upgrade finishes, so scouting
   one building tells you the next three minutes. Strategy is reading and hiding these clocks.
3. **Territory is income.** Bases deplete in ~10–12 minutes of mining, so you must keep moving out onto the map,
   where you are exposed.
4. **Upgrades change what a fight means.** +1 range or +50% speed turns a losing fight into a winning one, so
   *when* each upgrade finishes matters more than *whether*.
5. **Few, readable counters.** Each unit has a clear job and a clear answer.
